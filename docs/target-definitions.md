# Current target definitions and anatomical navigation

The viewer's five protocol targets are placeholders generated by `standard_targets`
in `src/targets.py`; they are not Brainsight anatomical targets. The seed is
the mean of scalp vertices within 120 mm of the highest scalp point. Each fixed
RAS direction below is normalized, a 400 mm ray is cast from the seed, and the
outermost scalp intersection becomes the contact. An outward normal is fitted
by SVD to the scalp vertices within 30 mm of that contact.

| Viewer label | Internal name | Fixed RAS direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMA | SMA | (0, 0.15, 1) |
| F4 | R-DLPFC | (0.55, 1, 0.35) |
| C3 | L-M1 | (-0.9, -0.1, 0.5) |
| C4 | R-M1 | (0.9, -0.1, 0.5) |
| Cz | Cz | (0, 0, 1) |

No cortical segmentation, hand-knob recognition, SMA/DLPFC anatomical selection,
subject-to-MNI registration, or Brainsight target import is used to place these
five contacts. The name/label mapping identifies the intended region, not an
anatomically validated location. Cz is also separately estimated as an extreme
scalp vertex for registration. The cyan EEG cap has its own arc-defined Cz.

## EEG cap is a separate construction

Cyan SMA is the Fz–Cz scalp-arc midpoint; cyan Cz is 50% of Nz–Iz; cyan C3/C4
are 30/70% of LPA–Cz–RPA. F3/F4/P3/P4 now lie at the intersections of
their transverse guides with Fp1–C3–O1 or Fp2–C4–O2. The transverse
midpoints are initial estimates, with reconciliation shifts reported in EEG QC.
This differs from the workbook calculator's standalone Fz–F8 midpoint rule.

## Brainsight anatomical workflow

Rogue Research documents anatomical targets as locations chosen visually on MRI
or 3D brain views, with target orientation estimated from the selected reference
surface and adjustable by the operator. In particular, a skin reference can help
set a coil orientation appropriate to the scalp above a cortical target.
The software records the selected target; it does not establish a universal
anatomical SMA/F4/C3/C4/Cz coordinate set.

Reference: [Brainsight user manual, anatomical target selection, p. 74](https://www.rogue-research.com/wp-content/uploads/Brainsight-NIRS-2.5.4.pdf).
This is the vendor's NIRS edition, which documents the target selection controls;
it is not evidence that our implementation reproduces the NIBS software's internals.

To implement the requested anatomical workflow:

1. Define the cortical point for each region on the individual MRI (or on the
   MNI template for a template-only demo), using the study's anatomical protocol.
2. Record the cortical coordinate and approach trajectory separately from the
   scalp contact. Use a Brainsight target export as the most direct reference.
3. Convert its declared coordinate system into the scene's RAS mm frame; retain
   subject/template identity and provenance. Preserve the exported trajectory,
   rather than replacing it with a ray from a generic head center.
4. Intersect the chosen trajectory with the scalp and seat the coil at that
   entry, respecting the trajectory and coil-to-scalp spacing.
5. Validate coordinates, scalp entry and orientation against the same Brainsight
   project. Anatomical definitions and target/trajectory exports are required
   before we can replace the placeholders with an equivalent implementation.

The existing Monte Carlo cloud recenters noisy ellipsoid offsets on each
placeholder contact. Consequently, its displayed miss statistics quantify the
simulated scatter around that chosen center; they do not validate these contacts
against anatomical Brainsight targets or measure the EEG cap's anatomical bias.
